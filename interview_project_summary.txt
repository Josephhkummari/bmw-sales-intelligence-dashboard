BMW SALES ANALYSIS PROJECT - INTERVIEW SUMMARY

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
We built an end-to-end BMW sales analytics project using Python, PostgreSQL, SQL, and Power BI. The project analyzes 10,000 BMW sales transactions from 2024-2025 and focuses on revenue performance, pricing strategy, regional growth, customer behavior, and product mix.

The goal was not just to analyze data, but to answer business questions that leadership would actually ask:
- Which regions, models, and channels are driving revenue?
- Are discounts helping sales or hurting margin?
- Which customer segments are most valuable?
- Is the EV portfolio growing or losing share?
- Are delivery times affecting customer satisfaction?

2. BUSINESS PROBLEM WE SOLVED
The real business problem was:
BMW needed a clear view of sales performance across regions, models, pricing strategies, and customer segments so management could make better decisions on inventory planning, pricing, regional investment, and customer experience.

This project helps answer:
- Where is growth coming from?
- Which markets deserve more investment?
- Is price discounting effective or damaging profitability?
- How do product mix and customer demand differ by segment?
- What operational issues affect satisfaction and repeat purchases?

3. DATASET
We worked with a BMW sales dataset containing 10,000 transactions and around 30 variables.
Key fields included:
- Sale date, year, month, quarter
- Model, variant, segment, fuel type, transmission
- Region, country
- MSRP, discount percent, discount amount, final sale price
- Options cost
- Delivery days
- Customer satisfaction score
- Sales channel, financing type, customer type
- Repeat customer flag, trade-in flag

4. TECH STACK USED
- Python
- Pandas, NumPy
- Matplotlib, Seaborn
- PostgreSQL
- SQLAlchemy
- Power BI (planned dashboard)
- Git/GitHub

5. DATA CLEANING AND VALIDATION
We started by cleaning the raw data and validating it before analysis.
Important steps performed:
- Checked duplicate transaction IDs
- Validated date consistency between sale_date, sale_year, sale_month, sale_quarter
- Checked for invalid prices and discount values
- Identified missing values and applied business rules rather than filling values blindly

Important business decisions:
- loan_term_months was kept as NULL for cash/subscription cases because a loan did not exist
- customer_satisfaction_score was kept as NULL for non-respondents because missing survey data is not the same as a zero rating

We also created a data quality report to document the process and findings.

6. FEATURE ENGINEERING
We engineered important features to support business analysis and dashboarding.
Features created:
- profit_proxy = final_sale_price_usd - msrp_usd + discount_amount_usd
- discount_bucket = Low, Medium, High, Very High
- year_month = formatted monthly grouping key
- delivery_category = Fast, Normal, Delayed

Why these matter:
- They help in analyzing profitability, price elasticity, and delivery experience
- They make the data easier to segment in SQL and Power BI

7. ETL INTO POSTGRESQL
After cleaning, we loaded the data into PostgreSQL using a star schema.
We created a fact table and dimension tables:
- FactSales
- DimDate
- DimModel
- DimRegion
- DimCustomer

Why star schema:
- Good for BI tools like Power BI
- Easier querying and slicing by region, model, time, and customer type
- Standard reporting design in analytics projects

8. SQL ANALYSIS
We wrote SQL queries to answer business questions from the star schema.
Main analytical areas:
- Revenue analysis
- Pricing and discount analysis
- Regional performance analysis
- Product performance analysis
- Customer and sales channel analysis

We used:
- Aggregations
- CTEs
- Window functions like LAG and RANK
- Running totals and year-over-year analysis
- Grouping by region, model, channel, and customer segment

Important SQL topics covered:
- Revenue by year and quarter
- MoM and YoY revenue trends
- Top performing models by revenue
- Region-wise revenue contribution
- Discount pattern by model and region
- Repeat customer rate by channel
- Satisfaction by financing type
- Delivery performance vs satisfaction
- Revenue by customer type

9. EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS (EDA)
We performed Python-based EDA to understand business patterns visually.
Key questions we answered:
- Is revenue growing month over month?
- Which region generates the most revenue?
- Which models sell most and which generate most revenue?
- What is the pricing spread by vehicle segment?
- Which models and regions have deeper discounts?
- Does discounting affect satisfaction?
- Is the EV lineup growing in market share?
- What is the repeat customer rate by region?
- Does delivery speed influence customer satisfaction?
- Are there strong correlations between pricing and customer experience variables?

Key findings from EDA:
- Monthly revenue was stable with moderate growth
- North America and Europe were strong revenue contributors
- Asia Pacific was the fastest-growing region
- 3 Series led in units sold, but X5 led in revenue
- Luxury segment had the highest median selling price
- EV lineup share slightly declined versus petrol models
- Fast delivery had a small positive effect on satisfaction
- Repeat customer rate varied by region
- Discounting showed weak relationship with satisfaction

10. MAIN BUSINESS INSIGHTS
Some of the strongest business insights were:
- BMW sales were stable and healthy overall, with no major collapse in demand
- Revenue was driven by a combination of volume and high-value models
- The 3 Series was a volume driver while X5 generated more financial value
- Regional market differences were important: some markets were high revenue, others high growth
- Discounting was not strongly correlated with satisfaction, suggesting discounts may not always be the best lever
- Delivery performance mattered for customer experience
- EV adoption did not yet dominate the portfolio share, indicating room for growth and product strategy review
